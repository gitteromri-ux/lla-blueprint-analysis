1. Bottom line

Conclusion

The Blueprint sales did not just disappear from Meta's reports; they stopped in reality. The CRM shows zero self-service (online checkout) Blueprint sales from Sep 17 to Oct 6, so broken tracking did not hide a stream of sales (VERIFIED). But tracking was badly broken from Sep 17 to Oct 4 (blackout, test-event flood, triple-counted purchases), which left Meta's algorithm learning from garbage exactly while the team edited the winning ad set, doubled its budget, then replaced it (VERIFIED events; their combined effect is ESTIMATE). From October the budgets were cut to $25 to $110 per day across three new ad sets, which is too little for Meta to deliver or learn, so results could not recover (VERIFIED). The September "streak" itself was only 3 sales at a cost of $1,379 each, so part of it may have been luck (ESTIMATE).

Labels used on every number: VERIFIED means read directly from Meta, the CRM, GitHub or a dated log; ESTIMATE means a judgment or calculation built on those facts.

4. Causes, ranked

Tracking broke right after the sales

From Sep 17 to Oct 4 the pixel had a total blackout (Sep 19), a test-event flood (Sep 20 to 24), impossible numbers (more page views than clicks, Sep 21 to 25), fake purchase spikes (15 on Sep 24 with no real sale), and triple-counted purchases (Sep 28 to Oct 4). During exactly this window Meta was asked to find buyers for the winning ad set at double the budget (VERIFIED).

  1. Meta's algorithm was fed corrupted signals while the winner was being scaled (confidence: high that it contributed, medium on size).
    • Evidence: blackout, test flood, purchase spikes and triple counting listed above (VERIFIED).
    • Effect: Meta optimizes toward whoever fires purchase, checkout and add to cart events. With fake or missing events it learns the wrong people; CPM fell to about $20 and click rate fell, consistent with cheaper, wrong traffic (VERIFIED numbers, ESTIMATE mechanism).
    • Limit: it did not hide real sales; the CRM shows none in that period (VERIFIED).
  2. The winning ad set was disrupted and then over-pushed (confidence: medium-high).
    • Evidence: Sep 16 audience removed and re-added, new value rule, location edit, then budget raised to $599 per day by Sep 20. The same ad set went from $2,730 for 2 sales (Sep 7 to 16) to $4,177 for 0 sales (Sep 17 to 28) (VERIFIED).
    • Meaning: each significant edit restarts Meta's learning; doubling budget in days pushes delivery to weaker audiences (ESTIMATE, standard Meta behaviour).
  3. October budgets were too small for the system to work at all (confidence: high for October).
    • Evidence: $25 to $110 per day spread over three ad sets; about 200 to 600 impressions per day; CPM $150 to $460 (VERIFIED).
    • At September's cost per sale ($1,379), this budget buys less than one sale every two weeks, and Meta needs about 50 conversions per ad set per week to leave learning (ESTIMATE).
  4. The winner was replaced by a different setup with no history (confidence: medium).
    • Evidence: Sep 28 replacement changed location (34 states to all US), age (35-64 to 45-64), interests (11 plus top-income filter to 41, no income filter), placements (automatic to feed and Reels only) and attribution (VERIFIED). The winning creatives were copies with new IDs; the original video copy went inactive Oct 10 (VERIFIED).
  5. The landing page and checkout changed many times right at the turning point (confidence: low-medium, unproven).
    • Evidence: 70+ commits Sep 15 to 24, including the v58 checkout rebuild (Sep 16 03:07), new hero page (Sep 16 11:06), WhatsApp widget (Sep 17), Google Pay added (Sep 17) and Apple and Google Pay removed (Sep 23) (VERIFIED, GitHub).
    • Against it: the Sep 16 19:41 sale happened after the v58 checkout went live (VERIFIED). Whether later changes hurt conversion cannot be proven from the data available (ESTIMATE).
  6. The September streak was partly luck (confidence: medium).
    • Evidence: only 3 sales; one was retargeting and one later cancelled (VERIFIED). With 3 events, the true rate could plausibly be anywhere from about 0.6 to 8.8 sales per $4,137 (ESTIMATE, statistical range).
    • Counterpoint: at the September rate, the $12,053 spent Sep 17 to Oct 31 should have produced about 9 sales; getting 1 or fewer by chance is about 0.2% likely, so a real change is likely (ESTIMATE).

5. Ruled out, and still unknown

Ruled out

The 'Update Required' flag did not stop the ads
Issue code 3858385 appeared only at the moment the 'Women +80%' value rule was saved (Sep 15 13:40, Sep 16 09:48) and cleared within 11 to 35 minutes, partly by Meta itself. It means 'the ad must be re-processed under the new ad set setting'. Nothing was left un-updated. The winning video ran 24,263 to 42,763 impressions per day Sep 18 to 21 (VERIFIED status history and impressions; meaning of the code ESTIMATE, Meta no longer returns its text).
The attribution change did not reduce reported sales
The replacement ad sets count 7-day click plus 1-day view, which credits more conversions than the old 1-day click, not fewer (VERIFIED settings).

7. Proposed rebuild plan

Proposal only

Nothing has been changed. These are suggested rules for Omri to approve or reject.

  1. 1. Prove tracking with one real purchase firstOne real paid order on a phone, then confirm: Airwallex payment, CRM deal, one browser and one server Purchase in Events Manager with the same event ID, and credit in Ads Manager. No test events on the live pixel.
  2. 2. Freeze the site and tracking for 14 daysNo checkout, page or tracking deploys during the test, so results can be read.
  3. 3. Rebuild the winning setup as one new ad setCopy old Adset 2's audience exactly (34 states, 35 to 64, 11 interests plus top-income filter, automatic placements) with the Julie 65-second video, Julie static #6 and Courtney static. Keep Adset 4 retargeting at a fixed modest budget.
  4. 4. Fixed budget, no edits for 7 daysSuggested $150 per day. No audience, rule or budget edits for 7 days unless a stop rule triggers.
  5. 5. Stop rulesStop at $700 spend with fewer than 3 add to carts and 0 checkouts started. Stop at $1,400 with 0 CRM-verified sales. Stop at $2,800 with fewer than 2 CRM-verified sales.
  6. 6. Scale rulesOnly after 2 or more CRM-verified sales at $1,400 or less each: raise budget 20% every 3 days; never more than 20% at once; any audience edit restarts the 7-day clock.
  7. 7. Judge on CRM, not Meta aloneCount only paid CRM deals created by online checkout ('Open By: ecommerce'), excluding test and QA orders and sales-team deals.

Thresholds are ESTIMATE, based on the September cost per sale of $1,379 and $7.50 to $18 per checkout visit. Note: $1,379 per sale is above the $755 to $895 contract value, so even the streak was not profitable on first-contract value (VERIFIED arithmetic).

Glossary

Pixel / dataset
Meta's tracking code on the site that reports visits, add to cart, checkout and purchase back to Meta.
Conversions API (CAPI)
The same events sent from our server instead of the browser; more reliable, but must be matched to browser events.
Deduplication
Meta merging the browser and server copy of one event into one, using a shared event ID. When it fails, one sale is counted two or three times.
Learning phase
Meta's trial period after a new ad set or a significant edit; it needs about 50 conversions in a week to stabilize.
CPM, CTR, landing page view
Cost per 1,000 ad views; share of viewers who click the link; a click that fully loaded the page.
Attribution
Which ad gets credit for a sale; '1-day click' only credits sales within a day of a click, '7-day click, 1-day view' credits more.
Value rule
A Meta setting that bids more for certain people, here 'Women +80%' and '2 placements +80%'.
